Fibre infrastructure planning & design

Route planning and design for new, expanded and optimised fibre networks across Australia.

From first route to build-ready design

We plan and design fibre infrastructure for every stage of a network's life: building new routes, expanding existing infrastructure, and optimising existing networks by providing new route diversity.

Each environment has its own constraints, approvals and risks. We bring route planning and design experience across the terrain and corridors Australian networks cross, from long-haul routes between cities to dense, congested metro areas.

Technical approach

Fibre selection. We choose the fibre to suit the job: ITU-T G.652.D single-mode for most routes, ultra-low-loss G.654.E for very long spans, and bend-insensitive G.657 for buildings and access.

Cable construction. Loose-tube, ribbon and micro-duct cable for underground routes, ADSS and OPGW for aerial and power-line routes, and armoured cable where it is direct buried.

Route diversity. A second route is only diverse if it avoids shared risk. We check for common pits, bridges, river crossings, conduits and exchanges that would take both paths down together, and design physical separation around them.

Loss budget. We calculate the end-to-end loss before building, using typical values of about 0.22 dB/km at 1550 nm, 0.35 dB/km at 1310 nm, 0.05 to 0.1 dB per fusion splice and 0.3 to 0.5 dB per connector. Try it in our fibre loss calculator.

Approvals and safety. Dial Before You Dig searches, road authority, rail operator and council permits, and environmental and heritage checks are planned into the design from the start.
